Working past the context limit

Compare ways to preserve facts and keep long tasks moving.

Keep a small working context, and keep the originals searchable. That combination gave the strongest results on our long-history tests. A keeper handles the notes and archive outside the working agent, so the agent can concentrate on the task. Run as a small proxy in front of the model server, for an agent that manages nothing, it answered 226 of 228 questions over a million tokens in 50 minutes.

A small desk, with the full history on file

Before old turns leave, the keeper saves them word for word and asks the model to refresh a short note: rules, current state and open work. It restores omitted rules and repeats them beside new questions. The model can search the originals whenever the note is not enough.

Choose what the task needs to remember

  1. For a long, changing conversation: keep searchable originals, short working notes and protected rules. Search finds things; notes keep the rules and the current state in view. A rule that names nothing it covers stayed in the notes (16 of 16) and was never found by search alone (0 of 16). On a harder test the two together beat either alone. Keeper and archive results · A rule that names nothing · Retrieval alone · The two together.
  2. For a ledger or repeated calculation: let code maintain the totals. Files and code finished the 121K ledger in 1.9 minutes with all answers correct. For prose, the model can submit quoted changes for code to apply. See both workflows.
  3. When the source fits comfortably: try supplying it together. Whole-history reading led on the 56-question chat-memory test. Compare with archive and summaries.

Summaries can work: they matched the archive on the smaller retention test, but took longer. A summary alone cannot recover a detail it omitted. Accuracy and time, side by side.
