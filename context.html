Working past the context limit

Which ways of managing an AI’s memory actually helped?

How can an AI keep working on one long job when its history no longer fits in its working memory? The same Qwen3.8-27B model ran each test below; within a test, every approach answered the same questions.

The best approach so far asks nothing of the AI agent doing the job. A “keeper” outside it moves the oldest turns, word for word, to an archive the model can search, has the model rewrite a short block of notes each time, and repeats the standing rules wherever questions arrive. Through a 32K window it answered every question on two 240K-token test histories and 226 of 228 over 1,000,000 tokens. How the keeper works, and what it scored.

Approaches that only drop or summarise old text forget the distant past, and none of them kept a rule that was stated once at the start.

Recommendations

What we would do today when building an agent whose history outgrows its window:

  1. Manage the context from outside the agent, and let the agent just work. When the conversation is about to outgrow the window, archive the oldest turns word for word, have the model rewrite a short block of notes (rules, state, open items) from the old notes and the turns about to go, and give it a search tool over the archive. This was the first method to answer everything on our rule-and-method test: 39/39 and 46/46 on two 240K histories and 226/228 over 1,000,000 tokens, with about half the model calls of the methods that make the model run a routine. It also runs as a small proxy in front of any OpenAI-compatible server (37/39 and 42/46; the second run exposed a defect that has been fixed but not yet run again). The proxy has only been run on this test so far, not under a real agent. The keeper.
  2. Give the rules and the goal a permanent, pinned place, and repeat them where they are used. Put them at the top of the context, where no clean-up step and no edit can remove them. With no such place, four methods answered 0 of 20 rule questions the way the rule required; with permanent rule lines, 5 of 5. Pinned only, a rule was still applied just 22 times out of 29 over 1,000,000 tokens; pinned and repeated next to each set of questions, 29 out of 29. Three lessons from failed runs. Outside studies found that a pinned head was the only compaction that kept rules intact.
  3. Put shared instructions in the system message and keep them unchanged, so the prefix cache can reuse them: a repeated 30K prompt started answering in 0.8 seconds instead of 11.4. How prefix caching works.
  4. Never let a summary be the only copy. Summarising at a threshold, as most production agents do, was the slowest method on a 240K history and scored below simply dropping old turns (23/29 against 25/29, one seed). Outside studies find each round of summarising a summary drifts further from the original. The keeper rewrites its notes at every cut too, but the original turns stay searchable and a rule the model leaves out is put back. The summary baseline · What summaries keep and lose.
  5. Drop tool output whose effect already lives in the environment, but keep the verbatim text searchable in an archive with a search tool. Dropping it outright lost every “who did it” detail. Archive and search versus compaction.
  6. Give a method that edits inside the window at least 32K tokens of room. At 16K the batch, the state, the instructions and the thinking did not fit, and accuracy collapsed.
  7. Measure with questions planted at known distances back, and include a rule. End-of-run questions alone did not separate methods: at a 32K window three of four methods answered all 35 final questions while scoring from 16/30 to 28/30 on the planted ones. And the method with the best memory score kept the rule no better than the worst. The distance tables.

What we are trying to solve now

An agent that works on one task for weeks, with millions of tokens of history behind a capped live window, and stays fast and on goal. The keeper now does this on our test histories up to 1,000,000 tokens, but only there: one family of test tasks, one model, one machine. Next come the proxy under a real agent on a real task, the proxy on the 1,000,000-token history with a plain 200K window beside it for comparison, and a second seed for the methods the keeper was compared with. The problem, the model’s limits and the plan.

The practical difference

Files and archives store the records. Active context is what the model can use right now. In these tests, that active numerical state stayed in GPU memory. How tools bring saved information into the next prompt.
