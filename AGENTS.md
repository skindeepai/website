# SkinDeep content and presentation

- Keep top-level pages short, plain and useful to a nontechnical reader. Lead with one clear takeaway and an obvious next step.
- Reveal detail gradually: overview, focused explanation or question, measured results, then original research files. Begin deeper pages with a short plain-language answer too.
- Reduce cognitive load. Avoid competing metric cards, repeated labels, decorative boxes and duplicate explanations. Keep qualifications and study setup beside the detailed evidence, not in the overview.
- Keep the actual approaches and results visible near the top in a compact comparison table, introduced by a short explanation of the test. Do not replace that comparison with isolated statistics or hide all results behind navigation links.
- Keep conversation history, agent activity and review commentary out of public pages. Include research limits when they change how a result should be understood or used.
- Use graphs and raster illustrations where they explain an approach, result or performance/quality difference more clearly than prose. Plot measured data accurately; distinguish calculations and illustrations from measurements.
- Visually inspect each changed page at desktop and phone widths. Check the reading order, spacing and chart labels as well as overflow and links.
- Keep image prompts, scratch plotting scripts and browser-test artifacts outside this repository, under the local Codex artifacts directory. Publish the final assets the site uses.
